I am a Nuclear Physics PhD student in the Nuclear Physics Group at the University of Birmingham, supervised by Dr Jack Bishop, Prof Carl Wheldon and Prof Tzany Kokalova Wheldon.

My research sits between computation and experiment. The core of my thesis is a Geant4 Monte Carlo simulation of $\alpha$-induced $^{9}$Be neutron sources (AmBe and PuBe) built around an analytical $^{9}$Be($\alpha$,n)$^{12}$C primary generator, together with the moderation and detector response that follow. Alongside this I work on detector-response modelling for LaBr$_3$ arrays and double-sided silicon-strip telescopes, a ROOT/C++ analysis framework to replace legacy tools, the digital acquisition system for the MC40 cyclotron, and analogue front-end electronics for portable detectors.

I have taken part in radioactive-beam campaigns at CERN ISOLDE (ISS, $^{7}$Be(d,p)$^{8}$Be) and TRIUMF ISAC-II ($^{9}$Li on boron), and I contribute to open-source scientific software — my rewrite of the Geant4-MPI interface was merged into Geant4 11.4.

selected publications

  1. NIM A
    Simulation and analysis of an AmBe source
    Filippo Falezza, Jack Bishop, Tzany Kokalova, and 4 more authors
    Nuclear Instruments and Methods in Physics Research Section A: Accelerators, Spectrometers, Detectors and Associated Equipment, 2025
    Abs DOI Bib

    This article details a comprehensive Geant4 simulation based on an analytical approach to the PrimaryGenerator class. Rather than simulating the interaction of 241Am alpha decay with 9Be through the in-built Geant4 physics list, this simulation uses the differential cross sections of the 9Be(alpha,n)12C reaction to directly produce neutrons and 12C ions while simultaneously increasing computational efficiency. The advantage of this is the ability to generate Doppler broadened 4.4 MeV and 3.2 MeV gamma rays from 12C decay and hence the verification of the excited states ratio per neutron emission. Each stage of the simulation has been verified against commonly accepted results, including the mass distribution of the 241Am fission fragments. The features of the emerging neutron spectrum have been compared to commonly accepted data, confirming the validity of the simulation. The source term and the simulation template are made publicly available in conjunction with this article. Although not extensively verified, the 239PuBe neutron source has also been implemented, making it a straightforward framework for implementing further sources.
